## Named ties and spoiler-safe timelines

The renderer’s tie key should be sorted as `leftId|rightId`; the labels below
are concise defaults, while the timeline text is intended for the reader’s
companion panel.

### Pierre and Andrei (`andrei|pierre`)

- **introduced 4:** old friends who recognize one another amid the artificial Petersburg reception; Andrei is amused by Pierre’s openness.
- **from 8:** Pierre admires Andrei’s will and judgment, while Andrei tries to pull Pierre away from the Kuragin circle and its dissipation.
- **from 50:** they are separated by war and circumstance; Pierre’s inheritance has made him a public prize, while Andrei is away with the army.
- **from 95:** reunited after about two years, they speak as friends whose moral paths have diverged; Pierre’s new Masonic ideas meet Andrei’s skepticism.
- **from 121:** Andrei’s love for Natásha becomes a subject Pierre understands before Andrei can fully explain it.
- **from 164:** Pierre acts as Andrei’s friend when Natásha’s rupture with Anatole is exposed, carrying painful information because Andrei should know it.
- **from 278 through 317:** after Andrei’s death, Pierre remains part of the family’s memory and Marya’s circle.

### Pierre and Hélène (`helene|pierre`)

- **introduced 2:** Pierre sees Hélène as the beautiful daughter of his host; attraction exists before either can name a relationship.
- **from 50:** Prince Vasili’s household and Petersburg society press them together; Pierre feels that everyone expects the match.
- **from 52:** married, though Pierre’s own doubts have never become a shared understanding.
- **from 72:** the duel with Dolokhov makes Pierre’s jealousy and disgust impossible to suppress.
- **from 74 through 234:** separated in practice but still bound by name, property, and public reputation; Pierre tries to live beyond the marriage.
- **from 235:** Hélène treats the marriage as a legal and social obstacle to another advantageous future; Pierre remains her estranged husband.
- **from 265:** Hélène’s death ends the marriage and leaves Pierre a widower.

### Andrei and Lise (`andrei|lise`)

- **introduced 2:** Lise is Andrei’s young wife, admired in society but left behind when he chooses military service.
- **from 26:** at Bald Hills, Lise’s cheerfulness sits beside Andrei’s reserve and the old prince’s severity; Marya tries to make her welcome.
- **from 28:** Andrei admits privately that neither he nor Lise is happy, even though he refuses to blame her.
- **from 75:** Lise is pregnant while the family thinks Andrei has fallen at Austerlitz; Marya and the old prince conceal the news.
- **from 77:** Andrei returns during the labor; Lise dies after giving birth, leaving their son and a family shaped by grief.

### Andrei and Marya (`andrei|marya`)

- **introduced 25:** adult brother and sister at Bald Hills, affectionate beneath the family’s strict habits; Marya knows Andrei as “Andrúsha.”
- **from 28:** Marya blesses him with an icon and asks him to be kind to Lise; Andrei leaves for war.
- **from 75:** Marya and their father grieve the report that Andrei is probably dead, though Marya continues to hope.
- **from 77:** his return and Lise’s death reunite them in shock; Marya becomes central to the care of the newborn.
- **from 278:** Marya’s last conversations with Andrei are tender, religious, and painfully direct.

### Old Prince Nikolai and Marya (`marya|old-prince-nikolai`)

- **introduced 25:** father and daughter live by his exact schedule; he teaches her mathematics and frightens her without being simply heartless.
- **from 53:** he distrusts the Vasili–Anatole proposal and insists on judging the suitor himself.
- **from 54:** his anger and Marya’s humiliation reveal how little freedom she has inside Bald Hills, even when he believes he protects her.
- **from 166:** wartime fear, illness, and political suspicion make their old pattern harsher; Marya must care for him while resisting his accusations.
- **from 278 through 317:** after his death, Marya carries the house, the family memory, and the discipline he imposed on her.

### Marya and Anatole (`anatole|marya`)

- **introduced 25:** the match is proposed in Julie Karagina’s letter; Marya knows Anatole first as a possible husband chosen by others.
- **from 53:** Anatole visits Bald Hills; Marya is flattered by his attention and imagines a possible happiness.
- **from 54:** Bourienne’s intimacy with Anatole exposes the courtship’s emptiness; Marya refuses the marriage and gives up the chance to leave with him.

### Andrei and Natásha (`andrei|natasha`)

- **introduced 121:** after meeting at Otradnoe, Andrei is unexpectedly renewed by Natásha’s vitality; she sees in him a serious, unfamiliar future.
- **from 129:** they speak plainly about love and become bound by a promise of marriage.
- **from 133:** engaged, but the wedding is delayed for a year by old Prince Nikolai’s demand and Andrei’s acquiescence.
- **from 151:** the delay and the Bolkonsky household’s coldness strain Natásha’s confidence.
- **from 160:** Natásha’s attraction to Anatole and her attempted elopement end the engagement; Andrei withdraws rather than forgive immediately.
- **from 164:** Pierre and Marya become the two people who understand the damage most clearly; Andrei and Natásha are no longer promised to one another.
- **from 278:** the dying Andrei and Natásha meet again in a relationship transformed by suffering and forgiveness.

### Pierre and Natásha (`natasha|pierre`)

- **introduced 18:** Pierre is a friendly adult guest at the Rostovs’ name-day dinner; young Natásha finds him funny and kind.
- **from 20:** he dances with her and sees the warmth of the Rostov household, while she treats him as a generous grown-up companion.
- **from 121 through 164:** Pierre is Andrei’s friend and Natásha’s sympathetic older acquaintance; he helps after the broken engagement without turning the bond into a romance.
- **from 318:** after the war, Pierre’s return brings him into Marya and Natásha’s circle; both women recognize how much captivity has changed him.
- **from 334:** Pierre and Natásha understand that affection has become love; Marya recognizes the change and helps them speak openly.
- **from 342:** Pierre and Natásha are married in 1813 and build a family life centered on mutual affection and children.

### Pierre and Anatole (`anatole|pierre`)

- **introduced 8:** Pierre is staying with Vasili Kuragin and sharing Anatole’s dissipated set, despite Andrei’s warning.
- **from 9:** Anatole treats Pierre as a strong, amusing companion in reckless drinking and wagers.
- **from 50:** Anatole is absent while Vasili uses the richer Pierre as a marriage prospect for Hélène; the Kuragin family’s social machinery replaces the old friendship.
- **from 162 through 227:** Pierre is connected to Anatole through Hélène and Natásha’s scandal; Andrei’s injury brings the two men into the same war, where Pierre later hears of Anatole’s death.

### Pierre and Platon (`pierre|platon`)

- **introduced 275:** fellow French prisoners; Platon’s stories, food, prayer, and unforced kindness give Pierre a human companionship unlike the abstract societies he had trusted before.
- **from 276:** Pierre’s attention to Platon becomes a form of gratitude and learning; Platon treats him simply as another man.
- **from 311:** Platon can no longer keep pace and is shot; Pierre’s grief is quiet, but the relationship becomes one of his permanent moral memories.

### Hélène and Anatole (`anatole|helene`)

- **introduced 2:** brother and sister in the Kuragin household, publicly presented as part of Vasili’s attractive family.
- **from 74:** Pierre remembers damaging rumors about their intimacy while confronting what his marriage to Hélène has become. Keep this late and indirect; the text presents it as rumor and recollection, not a settled early fact.
- **from 235:** Anatole is absent from Hélène’s plans for a new marriage, but the Kuragin family’s pattern of using marriage for position remains visible.

## Checking cautions

- The text names Andrei and Lise through other people before their direct scenes; c.2 is safe for Lise, while c.4 is the first direct Andrei scene.
- Marya and the old prince are both discussed in c.1, but c.25 is the first sustained Bald Hills introduction and is safer for profiles and their tie.
- Anatole is identified in c.1 as Vasili’s son, yet c.9 is the first sustained scene with Pierre; use c.1 for identity and c.9 for the reckless-companion profile.
- Pierre’s marriage to Hélène is narrated as socially inevitable rather than shown in a ceremony; c.52 is the first explicit “six weeks later he was married.”
- The later chapters use “Bolkonski” for both the old prince and Andrei’s family. Keep `old-prince-nikolai`, `andrei`, `marya`, and `little-nicholas` distinct in ids even when the display text uses “Prince Bolkonsky.”
