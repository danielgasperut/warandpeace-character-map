### Stage profiles

- **Count Ilya Rostov (`ilya-rostov`)** — 11–54: expansive, affectionate
  host and indulgent father, certain that hospitality and generosity are a
  family duty. 55–116: visibly shaken by Nikolai's wound and increasingly
  unable to keep the household's spending ahead of its debts. 117–240: gives
  up the costly marshal's post, lets Nikolai take practical charge at
  Otradnoe, and keeps the family's warm social life going despite ruinous
  finances. 241–341: during the Moscow evacuation he gives the family carts to
  wounded soldiers, sacrificing what remains of the estate. 342: arranges
  Natasha's wedding, then dies in 1813, asking forgiveness for dissipating the
  family property.
- **Countess Natalya Rostova (`natalya-rostov`)** — 11–54: affectionate,
  socially alert mother; she watches the young people's romances and is the
  emotional center of the house. 55–116: shares the family's fear over
  Nikolai's wound and later tries to discipline its spending. 117–145: worried
  about the family's finances and strongly opposed to Nikolai's imprudent
  marriage to Sonya. 146–190: reacts to Natasha's broken engagement with
  illness and alarm, then goes to Moscow to nurse her. 191–317: carries the
  household through wartime flight, Petya's death, and Natasha's grief. 318–353:
  widowed and dependent, she lives with Nikolai, Mary, Sonya, Natasha, and
  Pierre's enlarged family.
- **Natasha Rostova (`natasha`)** — 11–117: lively, impulsive youngest
  daughter, first absorbed in Boris and in the intimate play of the Rostov
  household; her attachment to Sonya is especially close. 118–130: at sixteen
  she meets Andrew again, falls in love, and accepts a delayed engagement.
  131–167: in Moscow she is impatient with the delay, becomes fascinated by
  Anatole, and breaks with Andrew after the attempted elopement is stopped.
  168–240: humbled and ill, she withdraws from society and begins to recover
  through family care and religious feeling. 241–317: in the evacuation she
  insists on giving up the family wagons to the wounded and tends Andrew at
  Yaroslavl until his death. 318–341: mourns Andrew and gradually returns to
  ordinary life through Pierre's companionship. 342–353: Pierre's wife and a
  devoted mother of children, now the practical heart of the extended family.
- **Nikolai Rostov (`nikolai-rostov`)** — 11–28: the count's eldest son,
  leaving university for the hussars because military service feels like his
  vocation. 29–68: cadet and then officer under Denisov; the battles expose
  both his courage and his fear. 69–106: welcomed home as a hero, protected by
  his father after the Dolokhov duel and gambling loss, then returned to the
  regiment with a more sober sense of duty. 107–145: loyal but financially
  constrained officer; he begins managing Otradnoe and openly wants to marry
  Sonya, while his mother resists. 146–278: serves through 1812, meets Marya
  at Voronezh, and goes to the Rostovs at Yaroslavl after learning of Andrew's
  illness. 279–341: grief and duty draw him toward Marya. 342–353: marries
  Marya, supports his mother and Sonya, pays the debts, and becomes a serious
  estate manager.
- **Petya Rostov (`petya-rostov`)** — 11–188: the adored youngest boy, eager
  to imitate Nikolai and impatient with the adults' attempts to keep him out
  of the army. 189–241: obtains permission to join a regiment and is thrilled
  by the patriotic life around Moscow's evacuation. 242–301: joins the army
  and seeks active service despite the family's fear. 302–310: serves with
  Denisov's partisan detachment, showing great enthusiasm and little sense of
  danger; he is killed during the dawn attack in Chapter 310. 311–341: remains
  present only through the family's grief and memory.
- **Sonya (`sonya`)** — 11–68: the Rostovs' orphaned niece, raised as a daughter;
  she and Nikolai are already in love, while Natasha is her closest companion.
  69–116: remains devoted to Nikolai and part of the household's emotional
  life; she refuses Dolokhov's proposal. 117–145: lives with the Rostovs in
  Petersburg and Moscow; she supports Natasha but accepts the countess's
  pressure against her own marriage. 146–167: sees Anatole's danger before
  Natasha does and warns her, helping Marya Dmitrievna intercept the plan.
  168–317: steadfast companion through Natasha's illness, the evacuation, and
  Petya's death. 318–353: gives up the hope of marrying Nikolai and remains a
  self-effacing member of the household; she moves to Bald Hills with Nikolai
  and Marya.
- **Vera Rostova (`vera-rostova`)** — 12–116: the eldest daughter, handsome,
  correct, and more socially self-conscious than the younger children; her
  sharp comments make her seem detached from the household's emotional ease.
  117–244: accepts Berg's proposal and becomes increasingly identified with
  his orderly, status-conscious world. 245–317: Berg's wife, still concerned
  with appearances while the family evacuates Moscow. 318–365: a married
  Rostov relation seen mainly through the family's later household references.
- **Boris Drubetskoy (`boris-drubetskoy`)** — 11–68: Anna Mikhaylovna's son,
  Natasha's childhood attachment, and a new officer preparing for service;
  his ambition is already more controlled than Natasha's romantic memory of
  him. 69–117: advances through military and court connections and becomes a
  measured acquaintance of the Rostovs. 118–130: returns to the family in
  Petersburg and gently closes the childish promise with Natasha. 131–229:
  an ambitious aide and court officer whose visits retain the old social tie
  but no romantic future. 230–337: remains a military acquaintance, including
  his meeting with Pierre before Borodino.
- **Denisov (`denisov`)** — 32–68: Nikolai's rough-voiced, warm-hearted
  squadron commander and closest military friend. 69–106: travels home with
  Nikolai and is welcomed by the Rostov family; he becomes attached to Natasha.
  107–229: remains a loyal officer and family friend. 299–313: commander of
  the partisan detachment in which Petya serves; his affection makes Petya's
  death especially painful. 346–353: retired general and familiar guest of the
  Rostov household.

### Linked stage profiles

- **Adolf Berg (`berg`)** — 18–116: precise young officer and Boris's comrade; 117 onward, Vera's accepted suitor and son-in-law, concerned with rank, order, and appearances.
- **Fyodor Dolokhov (`dolokhov`)** — 9–70: notorious officer and then distinguished reduced private; 71–84, Nikolai's gambling opponent and Sonya's rejected suitor; later Petya's daring partisan ally.
- **Princess Marya Bolkonskaya (`mary-bolkonskaya`)** — 25–278: Andrew's devoted sister drawn into the Rostov care for him; 279 onward, Natasha's ally and Nikolai's wife at Bald Hills.
- **Prince Andrew Bolkonsky (`andrew-bolkonsky`)** — 4–120: Pierre's friend and distant Rostov acquaintance; 121–167, Natasha's beloved and delayed fiancé; 168–279, wounded and reconciled inwardly with Natasha before his death.
- **Pierre Bezukhov (`pierre-bezukhov`)** — 8–167: awkward but trusted Rostov friend; 168–341, steady presence in Natasha's grief; 342 onward, Natasha's husband.
- **Anatole Kuragin (`anatole-kuragin`)** — 3–152: charming, irresponsible officer in the Moscow social world; 153–167, courts Natasha and attempts an elopement while concealing his marriage.
- **Marya Dmitrievna Akhrosimova (`marya-dmitrievna`)** — 18–167: blunt, respected Rostov friend and Natasha's practical guardian who intercepts the elopement plan.
